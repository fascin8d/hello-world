## 1. Theoretical Grounding — Three Positioning Frameworks

### Framework 1: April Dunford — Obviously Awesome (2019) + Sales Pitch (2023)
**Core idea:** Positioning = deliberately defining how you are the best at something that a defined market cares a lot about.

**The 5 components:**
1. Competitive alternatives (what customers would do without you)
2. Unique attributes (what you have that alternatives don't)
3. Value (the benefit of those attributes)
4. Target customers (who cares most about that value)
5. Market category (the context that makes the value obvious)

**Application to Circles:**
- Competitive alternative: Generic growth agencies, management consultants, or "we'll figure it out ourselves"
- Unique attribute: We start with what's already different about you and build systems that amplify it
- Value: Your team serves every customer who's waiting — without losing what made you special
- Target: Founder-led companies with unmet demand (R3 archetypes)
- Category: NOT "digital agency." NOT "AI consultancy." More like: **growth residency** or **operational amplification**

**Source:** [April Dunford](https://www.aprildunford.com/books), [Quickstart Guide](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)

### Framework 2: Andy Raskin — Strategic Narrative ("The New Game")
**Core idea:** Don't sell a product. Sell a shift in the world. Position your company as the first devoted to helping the buyer play a **new named game** — while the old game becomes obsolete.

**The structure:**
1. Name a shift happening in the buyer's world
2. Show that winners are already playing the new game
3. Tease the promised land (what's possible in the new game)
4. Introduce your offering as the way to get there
5. Show proof that it works

**Application to Circles:**
- The shift: "The companies winning in 2026 aren't the ones with the most tech. They're the ones who amplified what was already different about them."
- The old game: "Buy more tools, hire more people, copy the playbook."
- The new game: **"Liberate the growth that's already trapped inside your business."**
- Proof: Case studies of companies that freed up capacity, served their waitlist, and returned $250k in 9 months.

**Source:** [Andy Raskin](https://andyraskin.com/), [Medium: Name the New Game](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/to-create-a-new-category-name-the-new-game-70c2e55edc2e)

### Framework 3: Peep Laja / Wynter — Message-Market Fit
**Core idea:** Messaging has a hierarchy. If the top of the hierarchy fails, nothing below it matters.

**The hierarchy (top = most critical):**
1. **Clarity** — does the audience "get it" in 5 seconds?
2. **Relevance** — can the audience see it's for them?
3. **Value proposition** — does it show the "aha moment"?
4. **Differentiation** — what sets you apart from the alternative?

**Application to Circles:**
- Clarity: "We help companies that are too good to stay small." (Anyone gets this.)
- Relevance: "If you're turning down work or burning out your best people..." (ICP self-selects.)
- Value: "...we build the systems so your quality travels at the speed of your demand."
- Differentiation: "No AI pitch decks. No transformation roadmaps. 90 days. Measured. Your team runs it when we leave."

**Source:** [Wynter](https://wynter.com/solutions/about), [EarlyNode interview](https://earlynode.com/newsletters/achieving-message-market-fit-with-peep-laja-founder-wynter)

## 2. Case Studies of B2B Companies That Spread Without Cringe

### Case 1: 37signals / Basecamp
**Core positioning:** "The saner, calmer, organized way to manage your projects."
**H1 (Basecamp.com):** "Basecamp is a project management tool that helps small teams move faster and make fewer messes."
**The ritual:** Books (Getting Real, REWORK, Shape Up, It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work), blog, HEY World. 25 years of opinionated content. Zero paid acquisition.
**Why it spread:** They sold a worldview (calm over chaos, profitable over funded, small over big). Founders who share the worldview self-select in. Profitable for 25 straight years, zero debt, <100 employees.
**Lesson for Circles:** Sell the worldview first, the service second. The opinion IS the marketing.
**Source:** [37signals](https://37signals.com/00)

### Case 2: Linear
**Core positioning:** "The system for product development."
**H1 (linear.app):** "Linear is a purpose-built tool for planning and building products."
**The ritual:** Changelog (weekly, beautifully designed), blog phased from milestones → product philosophy → technical deep dives.
**Why it spread:** Opinionated product, obsessive craft, cult following that whispers about it in Slack channels. $1.25B valuation with a small team and almost no traditional marketing spend. In direct opposition to Silicon Valley's "fail fast" — they believe in craft, taste, and empowered individuals.
**Lesson for Circles:** Quality of the thing IS the marketing. The changelog is not documentation — it's a weekly proof of craft.
**Source:** [Linear](https://linear.app), [Eleken Case Study](https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/linear-app-case-study), [Thoughtlytics Growth Playbook](https://www.thoughtlytics.com/newsletter/linear-playbook-growth-engine)

### Case 3: Stripe (early days)
**Core positioning:** "Payments infrastructure for the internet."
**The ritual:** Stripe Press (books), Stripe Sessions (conference), Atlas (incorporation tool). Expanded the surface area of the brand far beyond payments.
**Why it spread:** Developer-first craft. The API documentation was so good it became a benchmark. They made a boring thing (payments) feel like a movement (internet commerce).
**Lesson for Circles:** The quality of your deliverable speaks louder than your positioning statement. If the residency reports are that good, they become the marketing.

### Case 4: iHeartMedia — "Guaranteed Human"
**Positioning:** Explicitly anti-AI in creative. 90% of listeners want media created by humans.
**Why it spread:** Bet on the authenticity premium at the exact moment the market was saturated with AI slop. A declaration, not a slogan.
**Lesson for Circles:** Take a specific, concrete stand. "We will never..." is more powerful than "We believe in..."
**Source:** [CNN Business](https://www.cnn.com/2025/12/16/business/anti-ai-backlash-nightcap)

### Case 5: Ahrefs
**Core positioning:** "Everything you need to rank higher & get more traffic."
**The ritual:** The blog. Hundreds of genuinely useful, keyword-research-backed articles that teach SEO while demonstrating Ahrefs' product.
**Why it spread:** They gave away the knowledge and let the product sell itself. Zero cringe because zero self-congratulation — just useful content, consistently.
**Lesson for Circles:** The content ritual should teach the ICP something valuable, not talk about Circles.

## 3. The Circles Narrative Architecture

### The Enemy
**"The growth-industrial complex."**

The old game: buy more tools, hire more people, follow the playbook, slap "AI-powered" on it, hope something sticks. The result: bloated tech stacks, burnt-out founders, teams drowning in platforms they don't use, and growth that comes at the expense of the thing that made the company special.

*Circles fights against growth that costs you your soul.* The kind that replaces craft with scale, and people with processes. The kind where "transformation" means a 6-month roadmap nobody asked for.

### The Promise (The Liberation)
**What's being set free:** The growth that's already trapped inside the business.

These companies already have demand. They already have a team. They already have something people want. What they don't have is the operational backbone that lets their quality travel at the speed of their demand. Circles builds that backbone — and then leaves.

**The promised land:** "Your team serves every customer who's waiting. Your founder gets weekends back. Your quality doesn't dilute as you grow."

### The Proof Type
**The 90-day residency report.**

Not a case study written by marketing. A transparent, data-first report published at the end of every engagement showing: the baseline, what was built, what changed, and what the client's team now runs independently. This IS the proof. And it becomes the content ritual.

### The Hero
**The client's team — not Circles.**

Every residency report names the client team members who now run the system. Circles is the coach, not the player. "We didn't do this. Your team did. We built the scaffolding and got out of the way."

### The Ritual
**The "Growth Liberation Report" — published monthly or after each engagement.**

A public-facing teardown/report showing:
- What the company looked like at day 0 (anonymised if needed, or with client permission)
- The bottleneck identified
- What was built
- The 90-day measured outcome
- Who on the client team now owns it

This is Circles' version of Linear's changelog or Ahrefs' blog. It compounds. It teaches. It proves.

## 4. Candidate One-Liners / Headlines

### Option 1 (The straightforward one)
> **"We help companies that are too good to stay small."**
Test: Specific to Circles' ICP. A founder who has unmet demand would read this and think "that's me." A generic startup wouldn't.

### Option 2 (The problem-first one)
> **"Your demand outgrew your systems. We fix that."**
Test: Names the exact pain (R1 #4). Active voice. Concrete. Couldn't describe a generic agency.

### Option 3 (The anti-agency one)
> **"No AI pitch decks. No transformation roadmaps. Just your team, amplified."**
Test: Explicitly anti-hype (R2). Contrarian. The "no" statements do the positioning work.

### Option 4 (The outcomes one)
> **"90 days. Your team. Measured outcomes. We build it, you run it."**
Test: Time-boxed, team-first, measurable, independence-focused. Every word does work.

### Option 5 (The movement one)
> **"Liberating the growth trapped inside businesses that do things differently."**
Test: Closest to the full mission. More narrative than punchy. Better as a tagline under a shorter H1.

**Strongest recommendation:** Option 1 as the H1, Option 5 as the sub-tagline, Option 4 as the "how it works" line.

## 6. Anti-Pattern List — "We Will Never..."

1. We will never lead with AI in our positioning. Tech is the engine room, not the showroom.
2. We will never call ourselves a "digital transformation agency."
3. We will never present a 6-month roadmap before proving value in 90 days.
4. We will never write a case study that names Circles as the hero. The client's team is always the hero.
5. We will never use stock photography of people in suits looking at screens.
6. We will never say "we unlock your potential" — we build systems, not slogans.
7. We will never take a client who doesn't have existing demand. No demand = no lever.
8. We will never leave a client dependent on us. Independence is the deliverable.
9. We will never use "synergy", "leverage", "empower", or "seamless" in any client-facing material.
10. We will never publish a piece of content that talks about us more than it teaches the reader something useful.
