## 2. RDTI Deep Model — Three Scenarios

### Background (from search data + Loop 1)
- Companies <$20M turnover: **refundable offset of 43.5%** (18.5pp above company tax rate)
- Companies ≥$20M: non-refundable offset, rate scales with R&D intensity
- Software R&D eligibility: must involve "experimental activities whose outcome cannot be known or determined in advance"
- Eligible software: novel system architectures, custom automation with unknown outcomes, AI/ML applications
- Ineligible: routine bug fixes, standard web/app development, predictable outcomes using known methods
- Registration: within 10 months of end of financial year via Industry Innovation and Science Australia
- **June 2024:** Updated "Software-related activities and the R&D Tax Incentive" guidance released by DISR

### The Body by Michael ruling (2025) — CAUTIONARY
The Administrative Review Tribunal ruled against a taxpayer because:
1. Could not prove the outcome was unknown
2. No hypothesis formed at the outset
3. Did not describe the experiment or establish the knowledge gap

**Lesson for Circles:** Every engagement that claims RDTI eligibility MUST have a documented hypothesis, experiment design, and knowledge gap statement from day 1. This is not optional paperwork — it's the difference between a $32k rebate and an ATO audit.
**Source:** [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/incentives-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive)

### Three Scenarios

#### Scenario 1: Low R&D Intensity (Process Redesign + Light Automation)
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Engagement fee | $250,000 |
| R&D eligible proportion | 20% |
| Eligible R&D spend | $50,000 |
| RDTI offset (43.5%) | $21,750 |
| **Net client cost** | **$228,250** |
| Savings vs gross fee | 8.7% |

*Applies to:* Engagements focused on operational process redesign with some custom tooling. Lowest defensibility — the "experiment" is harder to prove.

#### Scenario 2: Medium R&D Intensity (Custom Platform / Automation Build)
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Engagement fee | $250,000 |
| R&D eligible proportion | 45% |
| Eligible R&D spend | $112,500 |
| RDTI offset (43.5%) | $48,938 |
| **Net client cost** | **$201,063** |
| Savings vs gross fee | 19.6% |

*Applies to:* Engagements building custom software platforms, workflow automation with uncertain technical approaches, or novel integration architectures. Most Circles engagements would fall here.

#### Scenario 3: High R&D Intensity (AI/ML or Novel System)
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Engagement fee | $250,000 |
| R&D eligible proportion | 70% |
| Eligible R&D spend | $175,000 |
| RDTI offset (43.5%) | $76,125 |
| **Net client cost** | **$173,875** |
| Savings vs gross fee | 30.5% |

*Applies to:* Engagements building AI/ML systems, novel data architectures, or experimental technology where the outcome genuinely cannot be predicted. Highest defensibility but narrowest applicability.

### How Circles should use this in sales:
> "Your $250k engagement may qualify for the Australian R&D Tax Incentive. For eligible companies, the effective cost drops to between $174k and $228k depending on the technical approach. We'll help you document the eligibility — but we're conservative. We'd rather under-claim than over-claim. Your R&D tax advisor signs off before we include this in any proposal."

**Critical guardrail:** Circles should NEVER promise a specific RDTI rebate amount in a proposal. Always frame as "may qualify, subject to your tax advisor's assessment." The Body by Michael ruling shows what happens when you assume eligibility without rigour.

## 3. Refined Qualification Test (v2)

Loop 1 had 7 questions. This version tightens them based on R1-R3 findings and the ROI math.

### The 5 Gate Questions (Binary — all must be YES)

| # | Question | Why this gate exists |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Revenue ≥ $3M AUD annualised run-rate in the last 3 months?** | Below this, a 5% revenue lift = $150k, which doesn't cover the $250k fee. The math doesn't work. |
| 2 | **Is there a specific, observable signal of unmet demand?** (Waitlist, lead times >X weeks, documented turn-aways, capacity constraint the founder can name) | No unmet demand = no lever. We're amplifiers, not creators. If the demand doesn't exist, we can't liberate it. |
| 3 | **Can we identify ONE lever we can own end-to-end?** (Conversion, capacity/throughput, retention, or pricing — and Circles gets full control of the system for that lever) | Attribution disputes kill outcome-based pricing (Loop 1, Failure #3). If we can't control the lever, we can't prove the result. |
| 4 | **Will the founder AND an operational lead commit to a weekly 30-min check-in for 90 days?** | Engagement without founder access fails. But founder-only also fails (founder burnout is the problem — we need the ops person who'll run the system after we leave). |
| 5 | **Will the client sign a baseline agreement on day 1?** (Agreed metric, measurement method, attribution model, timeline — signed by both parties) | This is the contract's foundation. No baseline = no success fee = no proof = no Bottleneck Teardown = no marketing value. Non-negotiable. |

### 3 Bonus Questions (Preferred but not gates)

| # | Question | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Is the company eligible for the RDTI? (<$20M turnover, eligible activities) | Reduces effective client cost by 9–30%. Strong selling tool but not a dealbreaker. |
| 7 | Does the founder understand we build systems their team runs — we don't become a permanent dependency? | Filters out "we want an outsourced team" buyers who'll resist knowledge transfer. |
| 8 | Has the founder previously invested $50k+ in professional services? | De-risks the buying process. First-time buyers at $250k often stall, renegotiate, or get buyer's remorse. |

## 4. Rebate Reserve Model

### Assumptions
- 30 engagements over 9 months
- Fee structure: $150k base + $100k success fee
- Success fee trigger: client achieves $250k incremental revenue within 9 months of engagement start
- If success fee is NOT triggered: Circles does not earn the $100k (it's not a rebate — it's simply not earned)
- Question: what % of engagements will NOT trigger the success fee?

### Failure Rate Estimation

| Scenario | Success fee trigger rate | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Optimistic** | 90% (3 of 30 don't trigger) | Strong qualification test, narrow ICP, Circles controls the lever end-to-end |
| **Base case** | 80% (6 of 30 don't trigger) | Some clients underperform due to external factors (market downturn, key person leaves, client execution gap) |
| **Pessimistic** | 65% (10.5 of 30 don't trigger) | Weak Cohort 1, qualification test not yet refined, attribution disputes |

### Financial Impact

| Scenario | Base fee revenue (30 × $150k) | Success fee earned | Total revenue | vs Target ($7.5M) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Optimistic (90%)** | $4,500,000 | $2,700,000 (27 × $100k) | **$7,200,000** | 96% of target |
| **Base case (80%)** | $4,500,000 | $2,400,000 (24 × $100k) | **$6,900,000** | 92% of target |
| **Pessimistic (65%)** | $4,500,000 | $1,950,000 (19.5 × $100k) | **$6,450,000** | 86% of target |

### Cash Flow Timing (This is the real risk)

The success fee for engagement #1 (signed month 1) isn't earned until month 10 at earliest. This creates a cash flow gap:

| Month | Engagements started (cumulative) | Base fees collected (cumulative) | Success fees collected (cumulative) | Net cash position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | $150k (1st instalment × 3) | $0 | $150k |
| 3 | 10 | $900k | $0 | $900k |
| 6 | 20 | $2,250k | $0 | $2,250k |
| 9 | 30 | $4,500k | $0 | $4,500k |
| 10 | 30 | $4,500k | $300k (first 3 trigger) | $4,800k |
| 12 | 30 | $4,500k | $1,000k | $5,500k |
| 15 | 30 | $4,500k | $2,000k | $6,500k |
| 18 | 30 | $4,500k | $2,400k (base case) | $6,900k |

**Key insight:** Circles doesn't see any success fee revenue until month 10 at the earliest. The business must be self-sustaining on the $150k base fee alone for the first 9 months.

### Can Circles sustain on $150k base per engagement?

| Cost item | Per engagement (estimated) |
|---|---|
| Lead consultant (90 days @ $2k/day, 60% utilisation) | $108,000 |
| Supporting specialist (30 days @ $1.5k/day) | $45,000 |
| Overhead allocation (tools, admin, insurance) | $15,000 |
| **Total delivery cost** | **$168,000** |

**At $150k base, Circles loses $18k per engagement before the success fee.** This means:
1. Either delivery costs must come down (fewer consultant days, more efficient methodology, or junior + senior mix)
2. Or the base fee must increase to $175k–$180k (with $70k–$75k success fee to maintain $250k total)
3. Or Circles needs runway/reserves to cover the deficit until success fees flow

### Revised pricing recommendation

| Structure | Amount | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Base fee** | $175,000 | Covers delivery cost + 4% margin. Sustainable without success fee. |
| **Success fee** | $75,000 | Earned when client achieves agreed outcome. Still meaningful proof of ROI. |
| **Total** | $250,000 | Same headline number. Healthier unit economics. |

This is a pushback on Loop 1's $150k/$100k split. **The original split creates a cash flow trap.** At $175k/$75k, Circles is profitable on the base fee alone and the success fee is genuine upside, not life support.

## 5. What Loop 2 Killed or Weakened from Loop 1

1. **$150k/$100k split → replaced with $175k/$75k.** The original split loses money on delivery. This was the biggest gap in Loop 1's commercial model. The math was there in the PIPELINE_MATH.md ("model the failure-rebate reserve") but no one actually ran it. Now it's run, and the answer is: $150k doesn't cover costs.

2. **RDTI as a reliable selling tool → downgraded to "nice to have."** The Body by Michael ruling shows the ATO is tightening enforcement. Circles should never lead with "your effective cost is $165k" — it should say "may qualify, subject to your advisor." The RDTI is a bonus, not a pillar.

3. **"ROI guarantee" language → softened to "success fee."** Calling it a "guarantee" implies a legal obligation to rebate. Calling it a "success fee" frames it as earned upside. The psychology is: "we're so confident we tie our fee to your outcome" vs "we guarantee your money back." The first is bold; the second is a lawsuit waiting to happen.
