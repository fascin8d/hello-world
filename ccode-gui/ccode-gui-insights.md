# Circles — ccode-gui Insights: What's NOT in the Research Docs

**Author:** Claude (orchestrator)
**Date:** 2026-04-13
**Status:** Top-line strategic gaps identified after completing R1–R5 synthesis. These are the things that could make or break the 30 x $250k goal that the research doesn't cover.

---

## Operational / Delivery

### 1. Delivery team capacity
The docs model the sales funnel in detail but say almost nothing about who actually does the 90-day residency work. If you're running 10 concurrent engagements by month 6, that's 10 teams. At $150k base cost per project, your gross margin depends entirely on whether you're staffing with full-time senior people, contractors, or a bench model. **This is the single biggest gap.**

### 2. The founder time paradox
The GTM plan relies on you doing outbound, content, and closing — but you're also the person most likely delivering Cohort 1. If you're heads-down in delivery, the pipeline dies. You need a plan for who runs outreach while you're inside a client's business.

### 3. Scope definition for the 90-day residency
The pitch says "build the system" but the docs never define what a typical deliverable actually looks like. Is it a software build? A process redesign? An automation layer? Ops playbook? The answer changes your cost structure, team composition, and R&D Tax Incentive eligibility dramatically.

---

## Commercial / Risk

### 4. Cash flow timing
The hybrid model ($150k base + $100k success) means $100k per client is deferred up to 9 months. If you sign 15 clients in the first 5 months, you're carrying $1.5M in deferred revenue. Do you have the runway for that, or does the base fee need to be higher?

### 5. The rebate/guarantee risk reserve
If even 3 of 30 clients trigger a partial rebate, that's $300k–$450k off the table. The docs mention modelling this but don't actually do the math. You need a worst-case scenario on your balance sheet before you go to market.

### 6. Legal structure for the success fee
"Agreed metric hit" sounds clean in a pitch doc but gets messy in a contract. What if the client changes their business mid-engagement? What if they fire a key person? What if they stop cooperating? You need a lawyer to draft the baseline agreement template before Cohort 1 signs.

---

## Market / Positioning

### 7. Your own story isn't in there
The strongest founder-led B2B brands (37signals, Linear, Stripe) all have a founder whose personal conviction IS the brand. The docs build the Circles narrative but don't capture your origin story — why you, why now, why this fight. That's not something I can research. Only you can write it.

### 8. Competitive response
If "liberating growth" works and you start winning publicly, Josephmark, Humaan, and the mid-tier consultancies will notice. The docs don't address defensibility — what stops someone copying the positioning and the model? The answer is probably execution speed + the published residency reports creating a compounding trust moat, but it should be explicit.

### 9. Pricing anchoring against alternatives
A founder considering $250k with Circles is also considering: hiring 2 senior people ($300k/year), a Big 4 consulting engagement ($500k+), or doing nothing. The pitch doesn't explicitly anchor against these alternatives. Dunford's framework says you must — the buyer needs to understand what they'd do if Circles didn't exist, and why that's worse.

### 10. The Australian tall poppy problem
The docs reference the AU "ambition gap" (only 17% aim for global growth). There's a cultural dimension here — Australian founders can be uncomfortable with bold claims and public case studies. Your "Growth Liberation Report" ritual may need to be framed as "sharing what we learned" rather than "look what we achieved" to land in AU culture specifically.
