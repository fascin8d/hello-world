## The Core Thesis

Circles' ICP is NOT "companies that want to grow." It's a narrower set: **companies that already have more demand than they can serve, because the thing that makes them different is also the thing that's hard to scale.** The founder, the craft, the opinion, the relationship depth — it's both their moat and their bottleneck.

---

## ICP Archetype 1: The Founder-Led Craft Scale-Up

**One-liner:** "They built something people love. Now the waitlist is the problem."

### Profile
- **Revenue band:** $3M–$30M AUD
- **Headcount:** 15–80
- **Years in market:** 3–10
- **Ownership:** Founder-owned, possibly with angel/seed investors. Not PE-backed (yet).
- **Sector:** Design-forward DTC e-commerce, premium food & bev, wellness/health brands, specialty manufacturing, architecture/design studios
- **Characteristic:** The founder IS the brand. Product quality is non-negotiable. They'd rather turn away customers than ship something average.

### Public Signals of Unmet Demand
- "We're currently booked out until [month]" on their website or social
- Waitlists for products or services (check Shopify stores, booking pages)
- Extended lead times mentioned in customer reviews
- Hiring 3+ roles simultaneously (signal: scaling pain)
- Founder posting on LinkedIn about burnout, capacity, or "big year ahead"
- PR coverage mentioning "cult following" or "hard to get"

### Named AU Examples
1. **Koala** (mattresses/furniture) — DTC brand that grew to $200M+ revenue, founder-led, design-forward. Scaled from bedroom to national brand. ([1800d2c.com](https://www.1800d2c.com/locations/australia))
2. **Frank Body** (skincare) — Cult DTC brand, founder-led, Instagram-native growth, had to scale manufacturing to meet demand. ([1800d2c.com](https://www.1800d2c.com/locations/australia))
3. **Heidi Health** (healthtech) — #1 on Deloitte Tech Fast 50 2025, 15,323% growth. Pairs clinicians with AI to automate admin. Classic "demand outrunning capacity." ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/au/en/about/press-room/tech-fast-50-winners-pair-agility-with-discipline-201125.html))
4. **EatClub** — Featured on AFR Fast 100 2025, transforming hospitality with dynamic data-driven tools. ([AFR/Green Door Co](https://greendoorco.com.au/awards-showcase/afr-fast-100-afr-fast-starters/))
5. **Lorikeet** (AI startup) — $54M Series A, revenue up 10x since Oct 2024. Demand outpacing team capacity. ([SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/startupsmart/five-aussie-startups-excited-to-watch-2026/))

### Global Anchors
- **Glossier** (US) — founder-led beauty, cult following, waitlists
- **Allbirds** (NZ/US) — design-forward DTC, scaling challenge

### TAM Estimate (AU)
Deloitte Tech Fast 50 + AFR Fast 100 + Smart Company Fast lists = ~250 named companies per year with 3-year growth rates that signal this archetype. Broader universe: estimated 2,000–5,000 AU companies in the $3M–$30M band with founder-led, differentiated positioning. [UNVERIFIED — needs ABS cross-reference]

### Falsifiable Test
"If the company's growth is primarily driven by paid acquisition rather than product/brand pull, they are NOT this archetype."

## ICP Archetype 2: The Differentiated Professional Services Firm

**One-liner:** "They're the best-kept secret in their industry — and they're drowning in referrals."

### Profile
- **Revenue band:** $5M–$50M AUD
- **Headcount:** 20–200
- **Years in market:** 5–25
- **Ownership:** Partner-owned, often no external capital
- **Sector:** Boutique consulting, specialist law, accounting firms that productised, specialist recruiters, niche engineering consultancies, architecture firms, specialist medical practices
- **Characteristic:** Reputation-driven. Work comes from referrals. The problem is: every referral expects the partner's personal attention, and the partner is maxed out.

### Public Signals of Unmet Demand
- "We're not currently taking new clients" or long waitlists
- Google reviews mentioning "worth the wait"
- Senior hires advertised (signal: trying to clone the founder)
- Productised offering launched (signal: attempting to scale without the bottleneck)
- Speaking at industry events but no outbound marketing (all inbound, all referral)

### Named AU Examples
1. **SCD Advisory** — Independent corporate advisory boutique. ([SCD Advisory](https://scdadvisory.com/insights/two-insightful-views-on-the-consulting-market-in-australia/))
2. **Choice Chemist** — Disrupting traditional pharmacy, supporting 675 care facilities. AFR Fast 100 2025. ([AFR](https://ppam.com.au/2025/11/revealed-australias-fastest-growing-companies/))
3. **Squizify** — 12th on AFR Fast Starters, workplace safety SaaS, productised service model. ([Squizify](https://squizify.com/squizify-earns-12th-place-on-the-afr-fast-starters-list/))

### Global Anchors
- **Clearleft** (UK) — design consultancy, reputation-led, partners = bottleneck
- **Thoughtbot** (US) — product consultancy, productised their approach, scaled via methodology not just headcount

### TAM Estimate (AU)
AU consulting services market = USD $5.7B in 2025, growing at 6.58% CAGR to $10.2B by 2034. Government now reserves 40% of sub-$20M contracts for SMEs — opening the pipeline. ([Expert Market Research](https://www.expertmarketresearch.com/reports/australia-consulting-services-market))

Estimated 3,000–8,000 boutique/mid-tier professional services firms in AU in the $5M–$50M band. Of these, perhaps 10–15% are differentiated enough to be Circles' ICP = 300–1,200 firms.

### Falsifiable Test
"If the firm's growth strategy is primarily based on hiring more bodies (leverage model) rather than productising or systematising what makes them different, they are NOT this archetype." (They might still be a prospect, but the Circles offer won't fit.)

## ICP Archetype 3: The Operator-Led Specialist Business

**One-liner:** "They're the best at what they do — and the founder hasn't had a weekend off in two years."

### Profile
- **Revenue band:** $2M–$20M AUD
- **Headcount:** 10–60
- **Years in market:** 5–20
- **Ownership:** Founder/operator-owned. Often family or co-founder pairs.
- **Sector:** Specialist manufacturing, premium trades (fit-out, construction, electrical, landscaping), allied health group practices, specialist food producers, artisan agriculture, premium hospitality groups
- **Characteristic:** Hands-on. The founder built the business on personal quality. Every key decision runs through them. They want to grow but fear losing control = losing quality.

### Public Signals of Unmet Demand
- Lead times extending (ask in the first call)
- Job ads for operational/management roles (signal: trying to build a layer below the founder)
- Capacity investments (new facility, new equipment) announced
- Founder LinkedIn posts about "big decisions", "next chapter", or "building the team"
- Customer reviews: "amazing quality but took forever"

### Named AU Examples
1. **Intrepid Travel** — #2 on AFR Fast 100 2025, sales projected $630M. Grew from humble African beginnings to global powerhouse. Operator-led, values-driven. ([Intrepid Travel](https://www.intrepidtravel.com/us/intrepid-recognised-in-afr-fast-100))
2. **Wickton Properties** — #1 on AFR Fast 100 2025. Victorian developer managing commercial and residential from inception to final product. ([AFR/PPAM](https://ppam.com.au/2025/11/revealed-australias-fastest-growing-companies/))
3. **Firmus Technologies** — #3 on Deloitte Tech Fast 50 2025 (2,249% growth). Tasmanian, won Climate & Sustainability award. ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/au/en/about/press-room/tech-fast-50-winners-pair-agility-with-discipline-201125.html))

### Global Anchors
- **Patagonia** (US) — values-led, operator-obsessed quality, scaled without losing soul
- **RM Williams** (AU/global) — the craft manufacturing scale-up archetype

### TAM Estimate (AU)
ABS counts ~2.5M actively trading businesses in AU. Filtering for $2M–$20M, specialist/differentiated, owner-operator = roughly 50,000–100,000. Of these, the "unmet demand + scaling pain" filter likely yields 5,000–15,000. [UNVERIFIED — ABS microdata needed]

### Falsifiable Test
"If the business could be easily replicated by a competitor with more capital (i.e., the differentiation is price, not craft/quality/relationship), they are NOT this archetype."

## ICP Archetype 4: The Vertical SaaS / Productised Service with a Cult Following

**One-liner:** "Their NPS is insane, their churn is tiny, and they can't onboard customers fast enough."

### Profile
- **Revenue band:** $2M–$30M ARR
- **Headcount:** 15–100
- **Years in market:** 3–8
- **Ownership:** Founder-led, possibly VC-backed (seed to Series A)
- **Sector:** Vertical SaaS (specific industry), productised professional services (e.g., compliance-as-a-service, finance-as-a-service), dev tools, specialist platforms
- **Characteristic:** They own a niche. Their users love them. They have negative churn or strong expansion revenue. The problem is onboarding speed, support bandwidth, or the jump from "loved by 200 customers" to "used by 2,000."

### Public Signals of Unmet Demand
- Product Hunt / G2 / Capterra reviews with high NPS language ("can't live without", "changed how we work")
- Active community (Slack, Discord, forum) — a sign demand exceeds the product's self-serve capacity
- Feature requests piling up on public roadmaps (GitHub issues, Canny boards)
- Hiring for customer success, onboarding, or implementation roles

### Named AU Examples
1. **Honey Insurance** — #1 Deloitte Tech Fast 50 2024 (5,336% growth). Digital insurance. ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/au/en/about/press-room/tech-fast-50-winners-defy-economic-challenges-211124.html))
2. **Vinyl Group** — #2 Deloitte Tech Fast 50 2025 (2,374% growth). ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/au/en/about/press-room/tech-fast-50-winners-pair-agility-with-discipline-201125.html))
3. **Stropro** — #14 AFR Fast 100 2025, highest-ranking wealth-tech. ([Stropro](https://www.stropro.com/blog-posts/stropro-ranks-14th-in-the-afr-fast-100-as-australias-fastest-growing-wealth-tech-company))
4. **Sniip** — AFR Fast 100 2025, bill payment platform. ([Sniip](https://sniip.com/sniip-ranked-in-the-afr-fast-100-for-2025-blog-post/))
5. **Capspace** — AFR Fast Starters Top 100 2025, capital markets platform. ([Capspace](https://www.capspace.com.au/capspace-named-in-afr-fast-starters-top-100-for-2025/))

### Global Anchors
- **Linear** (US) — the canonical "loved by users, scaling the company to match demand" example
- **Plausible Analytics** (EU) — privacy-first, cult following, bootstrapped

### Falsifiable Test

## Anti-ICP — Who Circles Should REFUSE

### Anti-ICP 1: The Pre-Revenue Dreamer
- No existing customers, no revenue, no product-market fit
- Wants Circles to *build* the business, not *amplify* it
- **Why refuse:** $250k engagement with ROI-in-9-months is impossible if there's no revenue to amplify

### Anti-ICP 2: The Commodity Competitor
- Competes on price, not differentiation
- Would benefit more from a cost-cutting consultant than a growth agency
- **Why refuse:** Circles' value is in amplifying what's *different*. If nothing is different, there's nothing to amplify.

### Anti-ICP 3: The Enterprise Bureaucracy
- ASX 200, >5,000 employees, procurement processes measured in quarters
- Multiple approval layers, 6-month RFP cycles
- **Why refuse:** Can't promise or deliver ROI in 9 months when it takes 6 months to sign the contract. Circles' model requires speed and founder access.

### Anti-ICP 4: The Lifestyle Business (by choice)
- The founder explicitly doesn't want to grow
- Revenue is flat by design, not by constraint
- **Why refuse:** No unmet demand = no lever for Circles. This is a perfectly valid choice but not Circles' customer.

## The One-Sentence Buying Trigger

**"The moment a founder realises they are the ceiling — when demand exists but the business can't serve it because of a system, process, or bandwidth constraint that the founder can no longer solve by working harder."**

Common trigger events:
- Missed a big opportunity because "we just couldn't take it on"
- First key employee quit citing burnout or lack of growth
- Founder's partner/family said "something has to change"
- Board/advisors said "you need to systematise before the next round"
- Customer publicly complained about lead times or service delays
