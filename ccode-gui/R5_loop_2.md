## 1. Three Full Homepage Hero Blocks

### Hero Block A — "Too Good to Stay Small"

**H1:** We help companies that are too good to stay small.
**Subhead:** You've built something people want. You just can't deliver it fast enough. We build the systems that let your team serve every customer who's waiting — without losing what made you different.
**Proof line:** 90-day residency. Measured outcomes. Your team runs it when we leave.
**CTA:** Tell us what's bottlenecking your growth →

*Why this works (Dunford test):* Names the target ("too good to stay small" — self-qualifying), states the unique value (serve the waiting customers), differentiates from generic agencies (90 days, your team, we leave). Passes the "couldn't describe 100 other agencies" test.

### Hero Block B — "Your Demand Outgrew Your Systems"

**H1:** Your demand outgrew your systems. We fix that.
**Subhead:** You're turning down work, burning out your best people, or saying "not yet" to customers who are ready now. We build the operational backbone so your quality travels at the speed of your demand.
**Proof line:** $250k engagement. ROI in 9 months or we haven't done our job.
**CTA:** See how the residency works →

*Why this works (Raskin test):* Leads with the shift in the buyer's world (demand outgrew systems — a felt truth), positions Circles as the fix, introduces urgency via the ROI commitment. The enemy (the old game of "buy more tools, hire more people") is implicit in the alternative.

### Hero Block C — "No AI Pitch Decks"

**H1:** No AI pitch decks. No transformation roadmaps. Just your team, amplified.
**Subhead:** You already know what works. We make it work at scale. We find the bottleneck, build the system, prove the outcome, and get out of the way.
**Proof line:** Built for founder-led companies with $3M–$50M revenue and more demand than they can serve.
**CTA:** Book a 30-minute bottleneck call →

*Why this works (Laja/Wynter test):* Clarity — instantly understood. Relevance — "your team" and "founder-led" self-qualify the ICP. Value prop — "make it work at scale." Differentiation — the "No" statements do the positioning work. The negation is the hook.

### Recommendation

**Use Hero Block A as the primary.** It's the most emotionally resonant and the most shareable. "Too good to stay small" is the phrase a founder would repeat to their partner over dinner. Hero Block B is the strongest for a landing page targeted at high-intent prospects. Hero Block C is the strongest for cold outreach (it breaks the pattern of every other agency email).

## 2. The 90-Second Founder Pitch Script

> *Setting: coffee, no slides, across a table from someone who runs a company you admire.*
>
> "You know how there are these companies — craft-driven, founder-led, genuinely great at what they do — that hit a wall? Not a market wall. A systems wall. They've got a waitlist, or they're turning down work, or the founder hasn't had a weekend off in a year because everything runs through them.
>
> And every agency in the country is pitching them AI transformation. Some six-month roadmap they didn't ask for.
>
> That's who we work with. We call it a growth residency. Ninety days. We come in, find the bottleneck — usually it's a handoff, a process, or a system the founder is still doing manually — and we build the thing that removes it. Not a strategy deck. A working system.
>
> Your team learns it. We measure what changed against a baseline we both agreed on day one. And we structure the fee so we only charge the full amount if we actually delivered the return we promised.
>
> We're building our first cohort of ten companies right now. If that sounds like you — or someone you know — I'd love to have the conversation."
>
> *[pause — let them respond]*

### Script tested against three frameworks:

| Framework | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Dunford** (positioning) | Does it name the competitive alternative? | Yes — "every agency pitching AI transformation" and "six-month roadmap" |
| **Raskin** (strategic narrative) | Does it name a shift and a new game? | Yes — the shift from "tool-buying" to "system-building"; the new game is the residency |
| **Laja** (message-market fit) | Clarity → Relevance → Value → Differentiation? | Clarity: "systems wall" is instantly understood. Relevance: "founder hasn't had a weekend off" — visceral. Value: "build the thing that removes it." Differentiation: "we only charge the full amount if we delivered." |

### What the script deliberately avoids:
- The word "AI" (except to name the enemy)
- The word "transform" or "transformation"
- Any claim about Circles' team size, credentials, or awards
- Any technical description of what gets built
- Any pressure to buy — it ends with "I'd love to have the conversation"

## 3. THE Ritual — Named, Designed, Week 1 vs Week 12

### Name: **"The Bottleneck Teardown"**

*Not "Growth Liberation Report" (too self-congratulatory for AU tall poppy culture). "Teardown" is specific, technical, humble, and curiosity-provoking. It implies dissection, not celebration.*

### Format
A 1,500–2,500 word published piece (blog + LinkedIn) that follows a rigid structure:

1. **The Company** (1 paragraph) — who they are, what they do differently, why people love them. Named with permission, or anonymised as "a $12M AU design studio with an 8-week waitlist."
2. **The Bottleneck** (2–3 paragraphs) — the specific constraint stopping growth. Stated in the founder's words. No jargon. "Every custom order went through Sarah's inbox. Sarah is one person."
3. **What We Built** (2–3 paragraphs) — the system, the tool, the process change. Specific enough that a reader could almost replicate it. This is the "generosity as marketing" principle from Ahrefs.
4. **The Numbers** (1 paragraph) — baseline vs outcome. Measured. "Lead time dropped from 8 weeks to 3. Revenue per team member increased 40%. Sarah's inbox went from 200 emails/day to 30."
5. **Who Runs It Now** (1 paragraph) — name the client team members. "This system is now run by [name], [name], and [name]. We left on day 87."

### Cadence
- **Week 1–12 (pre-engagement):** Publish 1 teardown per fortnight using hypothetical or past projects. Frame as "here's what we'd do if we walked into a company like this." This is the MVP version — lower proof value but establishes the format and the voice.
- **Week 12+ (post-Cohort 1):** Publish 1 teardown per engagement completion. With real numbers. This is the compounding asset.
- **Ongoing target:** 2 per month once pipeline is flowing.

### Channel strategy
- **Primary:** Blog on Circles website (SEO value, permanence)
- **Distribution:** Founder posts the teardown on LinkedIn with a personal commentary paragraph ("here's what surprised us about this one..."). NOT the company page — the founder's personal page. Personal > corporate on LinkedIn, always.
- **Amplification:** Tag the client company and team members (with permission). Their reshare is the virality mechanism.

### Why "Teardown" and not "Case Study"
| Case Study | Bottleneck Teardown |
|---|---|
| Self-congratulatory | Curiosity-driven |
| "We did this" | "Here's what was broken and how it got fixed" |
| Vague outcomes ("increased efficiency") | Specific numbers ("8 weeks → 3 weeks") |
| Hero is the agency | Hero is the client team |
| Read by the agency's sales team | Read by founders who have the same bottleneck |
| AU founders scroll past it | AU founders forward it |

### Comparable rituals that worked

| Company | Ritual | Cadence | Compounding effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Linear** | Changelog | Weekly | $1.25B valuation, almost zero traditional marketing. "Cult following whispered about in Slack channels." ([Eleken](https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/linear-app-case-study)) |
| **Ahrefs** | Blog (SEO tutorials) | 3–5/week | Hundreds of articles that teach while demonstrating the product. Content IS the acquisition channel. |
| **37signals** | Books + blog | Ongoing since 2004 | REWORK sold 500k+ copies. The worldview IS the funnel. Profitable 25 straight years. ([37signals](https://37signals.com/00)) |
| **Atlassian** | Team Playbook | Ongoing | Made public 2016. "Just enough consistency, full autonomy." Extended brand beyond product into culture. ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook)) |
| **Stripe** | Stripe Press | Ongoing | Books about ideas, not payments. Expanded brand surface area beyond the product. |
| **SafetyCulture** | — | — | Grew from Townsville garage to $440M+ valuation by putting inspection data in frontline workers' hands. Renamed from iAuditor to SafetyCulture when the platform outgrew the feature. ([SafetyCulture](https://safetyculture.com/about)) |

## 4. AU Cult Brand Case Studies (New for Loop 2)

### Case Study 6: Canva (AU — early days)
**Positioning:** "Design for everyone." Launched 2013 targeting a hyper-specific use case: people making Facebook content who weren't designers.
**Why it worked:** Didn't try to be everything on day 1. Narrowed to one job-to-be-done (social media graphics for non-designers), nailed it, then expanded. The "everyone" in "design for everyone" was earned through years of product execution, not declared upfront.
**Lesson for Circles:** Start with one archetype (Archetype 1: founder-led craft scale-up) and dominate it before expanding. "Liberating growth for everyone" would be cringe. "Liberating growth for founder-led companies with waitlists" is specific and earned.
**Source:** [How Canva Grows](https://www.howtheygrow.co/p/how-canva-grows)

### Case Study 7: Atlassian (AU)
**Positioning:** "You've got to be odd to be number one" (Dr. Seuss quote in their IPO filing).
**The ritual:** Team Playbook — made public, giving away their internal operating practices. No paid sales team for the first decade.
**Why it worked:** They positioned as the anti-enterprise-sales company. The product sold itself. The Playbook extended the brand beyond software into "how teams work."
**Cultural fit for AU:** The "odd" framing plays perfectly against tall poppy syndrome — it reframes being different as an asset, not an embarrassment.
**Source:** [First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/unpacking-5-of-atlassians-most-unconventional-company-building-moves/)

### Case Study 8: SafetyCulture (AU — Townsville to $440M+)
**Positioning:** "Improve workplace operations" (current). Started as a mobile inspection checklist (iAuditor), expanded to workplace ops platform.
**Why it worked:** Built by a workplace accident investigator who lived the problem. The founder's lived experience IS the credibility. Renamed from iAuditor → SafetyCulture when the platform outgrew the feature — the brand followed the mission, not the product.
**Lesson for Circles:** The founder's story of why they fight matters more than the service description. Luke Anear investigated workplace accidents — that's not a positioning exercise, it's a life. What's the Circles founder's equivalent?
**Source:** [SafetyCulture About](https://safetyculture.com/about), [Business Model Canvas](https://businessmodelcanvastemplate.com/blogs/brief-history/safetyculture-brief-history)

## 5. Cringe Cautionary Tales (New for Loop 2)

### Cautionary Tale 1: Jaguar — "Copy Nothing" (2024)
**What happened:** Jaguar launched a 30-second rebrand video featuring models of varying ages, genders, and races — but no cars. Tagline: "Copy Nothing." The campaign was part of their pivot to electric vehicles.
**The backlash:** Elon Musk tweeted "Do you sell cars?" to his 200M+ followers. The video got 90M+ views on X, overwhelmingly negative. Consumers called it "embarrassing." A global agency review followed. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2024/11/21/do-you-sell-cars-jaguars-rebrand-prompts-mockery-confusion-online))
**What killed it:**
1. **Abstraction without substance.** "Copy nothing" is philosophy, not positioning. It could mean anything.
2. **Abandoned the existing customer** before acquiring the new one. Heritage fans felt betrayed; EV buyers had nothing to buy yet.
3. **Style over product.** Showed people, not cars. In a car company.
**Lesson for Circles:** Never position around a concept without grounding it in the specific thing you do. "Liberating growth" is dangerously close to "Copy nothing" if it's not immediately followed by "here's the bottleneck, here's the system, here's the number." **Specificity is the antidote to cringe.**

### Cautionary Tale 2: Liquid Death — UK Exit (2025)
**What happened:** Liquid Death, the US canned water brand with heavy metal branding and irreverent marketing ($1.4B valuation), exited the UK market in February 2025 after failing to gain traction.
**The backlash:** UK consumers saw it as "an expensive can of water with heavy metal writing on it." The brand that worked in the US (where water is boring and branding is valued) didn't transfer to a market where the premium water category was already established and the irreverence fell flat.
**What killed it:**
1. **Marketing without meaning.** When the novelty of the brand wore off, there was no substance underneath. "Just an expensive can of water."
2. **Cultural mismatch.** US irreverence ≠ UK/AU irreverence. What reads as edgy in LA reads as try-hard in London (or Sydney).
3. **Brand ≠ business model.** $1.4B valuation on marketing alone, without a defensible product moat.
**Lesson for Circles:** The Australian market is closer to the UK than the US in how it receives bold claims. The Bottleneck Teardown works in AU *because* it's humble and specific. A US-style "we're the greatest growth agency on earth" positioning would get the Liquid Death treatment — admired from a distance, ignored up close.
**Source:** [Coalition Technologies](https://coalitiontechnologies.com/blog/bad-marketing-strategies-2025s-most-obnoxious-trends)

## 6. What Loop 2 Killed or Weakened from Loop 1

1. **"Growth Liberation Report" → renamed to "Bottleneck Teardown."** The original name was self-congratulatory — "liberation" implies the hero is the liberator (Circles). "Teardown" shifts the hero to the problem and the solution. More consistent with AU culture and the "hero is the client's team" principle.

2. **Option 5 one-liner weakened:** "Liberating the growth trapped inside businesses that do things differently" — still works as a *mission statement* but too abstract for an H1. Demoted to sub-tagline. Option 1 ("too good to stay small") is confirmed as the strongest H1 by the framework tests.

3. **Linear as the primary model → shared with SafetyCulture.** Loop 1 over-indexed on Linear (US, VC-backed, software). SafetyCulture is a better primary model for Circles because: AU-founded, operator-built, started with a specific problem (workplace inspections → platform), and the founder's lived experience was the positioning.

## Top 3 Dig-Deeper for Loop 3

1. **A/B test the three hero blocks with real founders.** Use Wynter or a manual version — send all three to 10 AU founders matching the ICP and ask: "Which would make you click? Which would you forward?" This is the only way to settle the H1 question.
2. **Write the first Bottleneck Teardown** — even if hypothetical. Publish it. Measure reshares. The format is designed; now it needs to be tested in the wild.
3. **Record the founder reading the 90-second pitch script** and get 5 people's honest reaction. The script works on paper — does it work in the founder's voice?
